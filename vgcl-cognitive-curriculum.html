1.Abstract

Verification-Guided Curriculum Learning (VGCL) investigates whether failure-targeted verified synthetic curricula produce greater capability transfer than uniformly sampled verified synthetic curricula, under identical compute budgets. This document reports the frozen state of the VGCL_Pipeline repository based on a forensic audit of its complete history, through its V7 freeze on 11 September 2026.

The project built a two-environment pipeline: local generation, repair, and verification of synthetic programming problems, followed by remote probing of a small base model to measure observed failure, followed by local ingestion, target validation, and eligibility gating. Successive audits across the project's history found that earlier versions conflated requested failure targets with observed ones, measured difficulty synthetically rather than empirically, allowed treatment and control conditions to drift apart in ways beyond the intended targeting difference, permitted fallback-model contamination, and risked leakage between calibration and evaluation data. Later revisions addressed each problem individually; V7 consolidates the repairs into a single eligibility and provenance system.

The repository's authoritative test suite passed in full under direct execution, which establishes that the implementation behaves as specified for the properties those tests check. It does not constitute evidence about model capability or about the central hypothesis.

No treatment/control training-and-evaluation cohort was completed before the freeze. The central question remains open: does failure targeting outperform uniform sampling? What follows reports what the pipeline does, what its audits found, and what was never measured.

2.Research question and hypothesis

VGCL was built to answer one question: do failure-targeted verified synthetic curricula produce greater capability transfer than uniformly sampled verified synthetic curricula, under identical compute budgets?

The hypothesis under test is that they do: generating synthetic training problems aimed at a model's observed failures is more sample-efficient than generating the same volume of verified problems without that targeting.

Testing this hypothesis requires a completed comparison: a treatment cohort and a control cohort, matched on compute budget and pipeline quality, carried through training and evaluation under conditions that isolate targeting as the variable of interest. That comparison was not completed before the project was frozen. Nothing in this document should be read as reporting its result, because no such result exists.

3.Background and motivation

Curriculum learning and targeted data selection are established ideas in machine learning: the order and composition of training data can affect what a model learns and how efficiently it learns it. Pairing automated verification with synthetic data generation is a similarly established way to scale training data for program synthesis and code-generation tasks. Tests or differential checks certify that a generated problem and its solution are actually correct, so the resulting data does not depend entirely on human-authored problems.

VGCL combines these two ideas and asks a narrower, empirical question: given a verified synthetic-data pipeline capable of generating problems either uniformly across a task distribution or deliberately targeted at a model's observed failures, does the targeted version transfer better under matched compute? Pairing verification or search with iterative training is a related motivation in other machine learning research, in domains such as program synthesis and formal reasoning that differ substantially in scale, method, and verification model from the pipeline described here. VGCL does not extend or depend on results from those systems; it is a self-contained pipeline built to test one specific comparison.

4.Experimental design

The intended experiment compares two conditions under equal compute:

  • Treatment: a verified synthetic curriculum generated with generation deliberately targeted at failure clusters observed in the base model.
  • Control: a verified synthetic curriculum generated uniformly across a fixed set of algorithmic subtypes, without failure targeting.

Both conditions share the same generation, repair, and verification infrastructure; the intended difference between them is the presence or absence of failure targeting. A completed experiment would carry both cohorts through identical training and evaluation and compare capability transfer between them.

The frozen repository contains the infrastructure to generate both cohorts and the machinery to gate, validate, and track their provenance. It does not contain a completed run of the comparison itself. Sections 5 through 11 describe that infrastructure: what it does, and what it did and did not establish about the cohorts it can produce.

5.Final VGCL architecture

The V7 pipeline runs in three stages across two execution environments: local generation and verification, remote probing on Kaggle, and local ingestion of the probe result.

Local generation and verification
API generation→ Syntax / repair→ Reference-independence screening→ Subtype validation→ Phase A verification→ Phase B verification→ Structural deduplication→ Ledger / manifest staging→ PENDING_KAGGLE_PROBE
↓ manifest sent to Kaggle
Remote probing on Kaggle
Manifest→ Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct→ Pass@1 / Pass@10 probing→ Probe result
↓ probe result returned locally
Local ingestion
Probe result→ Integrity / provenance checks→ Target validation→ Primary eligibility→ Approved / auxiliary

Generation and verification happen locally, one candidate at a time. An API call produces a candidate problem and solution, which passes through syntax repair, a check that the reference solution was produced independently of the optimized one, a subtype-validity check, two stages of behavioral verification, and structural deduplication against prior candidates. Candidates that pass are staged into a manifest and marked PENDING_KAGGLE_PROBE.

The manifest is then probed remotely against Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct under a Pass@1 and Pass@10 protocol (Section 8), producing a probe result per candidate. Probe results return to local ingestion, where integrity and provenance checks run, the requested failure target is checked against what the probe actually observed (Section 7), and the candidate is evaluated against the primary eligibility gate (Section 10) before being routed to an approved or auxiliary pool.

The generator is not the model under study, and Qwen is not used to write problems. It only attempts them. No checkpoint resulting from training on VGCL curriculum data exists in the frozen repository; the pipeline produces and gates curriculum data, but does not include a training run.

7.Failure targeting

Failure targeting is the mechanism meant to distinguish the treatment condition from the control. It rests on classifying observed model failures into clusters, and then checking that a candidate generated to target a cluster actually elicited that cluster's failure.

Failure clusters

The pipeline defines eight failure clusters, used as labels for observed runtime behavior rather than as claims about internal model mechanism. The table below expands each label to a short reading.

ClusterReadingReachability note
FC_01_MICRO_CORRECTNESSincorrect output on small-scale casescan absorb unrelated small-scale failures
FC_02_SCALE_DIVERGENCEdiverges at larger input scalenot fully reachable via the current probing path
FC_03_TERMINATIONfails to terminate, or terminate correctlycan conflate slow execution with non-termination; hard timeout incomplete
FC_04_BOUNDS_VIOLATIONviolates input/output boundsnone noted
FC_05_RESOURCE_FAILUREexceeds memory or other resource limitsnot fully reachable via the current probing path
FC_06_FORMAT_CONTRACTviolates the expected output formatnone noted
FC_07_STATE_INCONSISTENCYinconsistent state across executionnot fully reachable via the current probing path
FC_08_UNKNOWNfailures not classified abovecan be shadowed by the current small-scale fallback

These are runtime categories: what a submitted solution did when it ran against a probe. They are not neural mechanisms, reasoning circuits, latent cognitive modules, or internal representations. Nothing in the pipeline inspects model internals. The reachability notes above describe limits of the current probing evidence path, not a claim that the clusters themselves are invalid.

Target validation

A more basic distinction underlies all of this: a requested target is not the same as an observed, validated target. Requesting that a candidate be generated for FC_01 does not, by itself, establish that the candidate actually elicited FC_01 behavior when probed. It establishes only that FC_01 was the intended target at generation time.

The intended validation path is: requested cluster → candidate → Qwen probe → observed failure → classification → target validation → eligibility. A candidate earns a validated target status only after its probe result is classified and matched back against the request.

What the audit found: merge_probe_results.py trusts the declared target-validation status attached to a probe result rather than fully recomputing it from raw probe evidence each time. Target authenticity is improved relative to earlier versions, which used requested labels directly. It is not, however, cryptographically or semantically guaranteed by the merge step itself.

8.Base-model probing

The pipeline measures failure by probing Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct against each generated candidate, under two protocols:

  • Pass@1: one greedy completion.
  • Pass@10: one greedy completion plus nine sampled completions, at temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95, k = 10.

difficulty = 1.0 − pass@10

Pass@10 in the current implementation is a binary any-pass result: at least one of the ten completions is correct, or none is. It is not a smooth pass-rate estimate. Difficulty is therefore binary at the level of an individual item. A description like "the model solved 70% of ten attempts" does not describe what this pipeline measures and should not be inferred from it.

The audit recorded several reproducibility limitations in the probing implementation as it stands:

  • the model-weight hash is not recorded
  • the tokenizer hash is not recorded
  • exact software-version identity is incomplete
  • GPU identity is not recorded
  • the complete RNG state is not recorded
  • the complete set of sampled outputs is not retained
  • the stopping specification is not completely explicit

These are reproducibility limits on the probing step, not indications that the probe results themselves are wrong.

Ordinary local execution is separated from base-model probing: the guarded prober blocks Qwen from loading locally by default, and probing is expected to run on Kaggle. Explicit overrides exist for controlled local probing, so the accurate statement is that the default path prevents local loading, not that local loading is categorically impossible. This separation is also not repository-wide: other tooling, such as calibration and trajectory scripts, can load Qwen directly, outside the guarded path.

9.Verification and candidate quality

Verification happens in two phases, and the audit is specific that neither constitutes formal proof: what the pipeline provides is behavioral verification under the tested oracle conditions.

Phase A and Phase B

Phase A differentially tests the optimized solution against a brute-force or reference implementation on generated small cases, using the same inputs and checking for the same expected outputs. Phase B runs the optimized implementation alone on larger cases and checks runtime, memory, and scaling behavior against heuristics.

Phase B does not compare outputs to a reference at this scale. The minimum case size for large-case testing is not strictly enforced, and the scaling checks are heuristics over the candidate's abstract syntax tree, not formal complexity proofs. Resource enforcement differs by platform, wall-clock timing can include infrastructure overhead unrelated to the candidate's own performance, and the execution sandbox is not hardened security isolation. None of this amounts to a formal proof system; it is closer to a differential and heuristic test suite applied per candidate.

Reference independence

Each candidate's reference solution is produced by a separate generation call under the generator policy, prompt-isolated from the call that produced the optimized solution. The pipeline then screens the two for structural similarity, rejecting pairs above a threshold of 0.85.

This is structural screening, not proof of semantic independence. Passing the threshold does not establish that the two solutions reflect independent concepts, that they avoid sharing the same underlying mistake, that they are free of shared bias from a single generation provider, or that no leakage occurred at the level of the specification itself.

Deduplication

Candidates are deduplicated using a normalized abstract syntax tree, a control-flow-graph representation, AST/CFG hashing, and a normalized four-gram Jaccard similarity over these representations, with a duplicate threshold of 0.70.

This has a narrower scope than "semantic uniqueness" might suggest. Corpus scope is in-memory per invocation, so deduplication does not span separate runs of the pipeline. Targeted and uniform generation runs use separate deduplication engines, so a candidate is only checked against others generated under the same condition. There is no demonstrated deduplication between the curriculum corpus and any evaluation set, and candidates rejected for duplication remain in the internal corpus rather than being discarded, so they can still affect later similarity comparisons within the same run.

10.Treatment and uniform control

The control condition is uniform in a specific, narrower sense than "uniform over all possible programs": generation rotates round-robin across six algorithmic subtypes, labeled A through F.

The control shares substantial infrastructure with the treatment condition, including the same generation, repair, and verification pipeline. It differs in prompt content, in the absence of failure-targeting instructions, in its subtype schedule, in its metadata, in its target-validation requirement, and in its seed range.

Eligibility gates

Uniform-condition candidates share the common quality pipeline with targeted candidates but follow a different eligibility path: uniform primary eligibility marks candidates with a control status of UNIFORM_CONTROL and a target status of NOT_APPLICABLE, and does not require failure-target validation.

Targeted primary eligibility is a substantially longer gate. A candidate qualifies as targeted-primary only if all of the following hold:

  • Phase A verification approved
  • Phase B verification approved
  • a structural hash is present
  • structural similarity below the 0.70 duplication threshold (Section 9)
  • no reviewer override applied
  • neither generation pass used a fallback model
  • the primary generator produced both passes
  • the generator policy matches what was expected
  • the subtype is canonical
  • target status is VALIDATED
  • the probe-result hash is non-empty
  • failure evidence is parseable
  • the subtype is compatible with the request

This is a gate on a specific scientific role: separating candidates that can support a targeting claim from those that cannot. It is not a general-purpose quality filter.

The audit's central finding here concerns what this machinery has not yet demonstrated: the repository does not contain a completed cohort showing that treatment and control candidates were empirically matched in difficulty, length, subtype distribution, or other realized properties. Consequently, "targeting was the only difference between the two conditions" is not something this repository can state as an observed fact. What can be said is narrower: the intended causal difference was failure targeting.

11.Provenance, leakage, and dataset integrity

Fallbacks

Generator calls can fail because of quota limits, rate limits, transient errors, or provider-side failures. When they do, the pipeline falls back to one of the alternate generators listed in Section 6. Because the optimized-solution pass and the reference-solution pass are separate calls, they can end up produced by different effective models for the same candidate.

Fallback use is recorded in candidate metadata, and primary eligibility rejects any candidate where either pass used a fallback. Archived runs containing fallback-contaminated candidates exist in the repository. But because no final primary cohort has been assembled and run through training, the repository does not demonstrate, empirically, that a completed experiment's population would be free of fallback contamination. It demonstrates only that the gate exists to exclude it.

Leakage

Earlier calibration data included material that overlapped with evaluation material, a leakage risk identified and repaired during the project's history. Current calibration data is derived from MBPP training data, and the earlier, contaminated calibration set is preserved separately in the archive rather than deleted.

An ID-overlap guard checks for shared identifiers between calibration and evaluation data. The audit's finding here is precise: ID disjointness is not the same as information-flow disjointness. Two datasets can share no identifiers while still sharing information through another channel, such as near-duplicate content filed under different IDs, and the current guard does not close off that possibility. No completed final training/evaluation cohort exists against which this question could be settled either way.

Ledger

The pipeline records experiment provenance in a ledger, with fields covering generation, verification, probing, and eligibility outcomes for each candidate. This supports provenance tracking: the ability to trace a candidate's history through the pipeline.

Scope: "Provenance tracking" accurately describes the ledger. "Immutable, authenticated provenance" does not. The ledger uses INSERT OR REPLACE semantics, so records can be updated in place rather than append-only; it does not enforce full relational foreign-key binding between related records; repeated JSONL records are possible; there is no complete hash over an entire candidate package; and prompt coverage in the ledger is incomplete.

Hashes

Integrity checks throughout the pipeline use SHA-256 hashes over canonicalized content. These are integrity and provenance hashes, not cryptographic signatures: hashing can confirm that content matches a recorded value, but it does not, by itself, establish who produced that content or authenticate its origin. No asymmetric signing scheme is present in the frozen repository.

12.Statistical analysis framework

The repository includes a statistical helper module, but no final scientific dataset to analyze. The module includes sample-size and power-planning functions that take design assumptions as inputs, such as an assumed intraclass correlation (ρ = 0.20) and the resulting design effect, to plan what sample size a future experiment would need. These are planning inputs, not measurements from any completed run, and should not be read as reporting an effect size, a power level, or a required sample size for the VGCL experiment itself, which was never run.

What the audit found: statistical_analysis.py labels a normal-tail calculation as a permutation-test p-value, even though the code does not perform a permutation test and does not use its own calculated Welch degrees of freedom to evaluate a t-distribution tail. This is a naming and implementation mismatch in the frozen helper code: a limitation of the statistical tooling as written, not a finding about any dataset, since none was run through it as part of a completed experiment.

This document does not correct that code or use its output as a statistical result.

13.What the audits revealed

The project's history is best read as a sequence of audits, each finding a way the experiment as built could have produced a misleading answer, followed by a repair.

VGCL began as an attempt to construct verified synthetic programming curricula. The initial generation strategy specified problems by algorithm, with prompts like "generate problems for algorithm X." An early audit identified this as distinct from, and weaker than, "generate examples targeting an observed model failure." Producing a problem that exercises an algorithm is not the same as producing one that provokes a failure a model has actually been observed to have.

Later audits found further problems: difficulty measured synthetically or by pseudo-random assignment rather than by observed model behavior; targeting based on requested labels rather than validated observed failures; subtype drift between what was requested and what was generated; confounding between treatment and control beyond the intended targeting difference; contamination from fallback generators; leakage between calibration and evaluation data; and provenance binding too weak to fully trust downstream.

Each of these was addressed in a subsequent revision rather than left as a caveat. V7 is the version that consolidates the repairs: the two-stage local/Kaggle architecture in Section 5 and the centralized eligibility and provenance machinery in Sections 10 and 11 both date to this version.

The project was frozen on 11 September 2026, before the treatment/control training-and-evaluation comparison that would have tested the central hypothesis was run. The methodological repairs are complete relative to the problems that prompted them. The experiment they were built to support is not.

14.Final frozen state

The frozen repository is tazwaryayyyy/VGCL_Pipeline. The V7 freeze tag, phase0-experiment-freeze-v7, resolves to commit 98398d6e49736d83fa3c39560d4d556a8a760419. The main branch has since received a documentation-only commit, acbea49, which does not modify the frozen implementation. The freeze date is 11 September 2026, and the repository's current status is frozen and archived.

The authoritative test suite, test_phase0_pipeline.py, contains 77 tests. Run directly under unittest, all 77 passed. This is a narrower claim than "all tests pass": the repository has legacy test-discovery complications elsewhere, and pytest is not available in the audit environment, so the finding is specifically about direct unittest execution of this one authoritative suite, not about every test-like artifact in the repository.

What 77/77 establishes is that the properties those tests check, which concern implementation behavior and not model behavior, hold as specified. It says nothing about Qwen's failure rates, about targeting effectiveness, or about the central hypothesis. Software tests and empirical model results are different kinds of evidence, and this document does not treat one as a substitute for the other.

16.Scientific status

The central hypothesis, that failure-targeted verified synthetic curricula transfer capability better than uniformly sampled verified synthetic curricula under identical compute, was not tested to completion. This document, and the repository it describes, does not support any of the following claims:

  • that VGCL improved capability
  • that VGCL failed to improve capability
  • that VGCL had no effect
  • that targeting was superior to uniform sampling
  • that targeting was inferior to uniform sampling
  • a statistically significant result in either direction
  • a measured effect size
  • a confidence interval
  • successful generalization
  • mechanistic confirmation of any kind

This is not a weakness to disguise. It is the scientific boundary of a project that was frozen at a specific point in its development, with its infrastructure complete and its decisive experiment not yet run. What the repository does support is narrower, and within its scope, real: a working two-environment pipeline, a set of methodological problems identified and repaired across several revisions, and a precise account of what would still need to happen for the central question to receive an answer.

17.Repository and reproducibility

The frozen implementation is available at the phase0-experiment-freeze-v7 tag (commit 98398d6e49736d83fa3c39560d4d556a8a760419) in tazwaryayyyy/VGCL_Pipeline. Reproducing the software-level claims in this document (the 77/77 test result, the pipeline's gating logic, the structure of the ledger) requires only that tag and a direct unittest run of test_phase0_pipeline.py.

Reproducing the probing results is a different matter, and the limitations described in Section 8 apply directly: without a recorded model-weight hash, tokenizer hash, complete software-version identity, GPU identity, full RNG state, complete output set, and explicit stopping specification, an independent run of the Kaggle probe stage should be expected to approximate the original probing conditions rather than reproduce them exactly.

No trained model, and no completed treatment/control cohort, exists to reproduce, because neither was produced before the freeze.

A working, tested, two-environment pipeline exists at the V7 tag. Each audit in the project's history found a specific way the experiment could have produced a misleading answer, and each was repaired in a later revision. No result exists, and the central hypothesis about failure-targeted versus uniform curricula is exactly as open as it was before the pipeline was built. If the comparison runs now, it runs on infrastructure built specifically not to repeat the mistakes this history catalogs.
