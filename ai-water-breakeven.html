1.Abstract

Evaluating whether water savings or supply gains linked to artificial intelligence (AI) can offset the operational water footprint of AI compute requires separating physical scale limits from unmeasured counterfactual assumptions. This study implements a 50-year global volumetric water-balance scenario model combining agricultural consumptive-water displacement and desalinated freshwater supply credits against the operational water consumption of AI compute (2027 baseline reference followed by annual compounding steps from 2028 through 2077), evaluated over 100,000 Latin hypercube samples (seed = 42). Operational Scope 1 and Scope 2 compute consumption starts at 0.38-0.60 km³/yr in 2027 and compounds under a two-phase trajectory against crop blue-water displacement via solar-driven microbial food production (1,080 km³/yr central agricultural baseline) and coastal desalinated supply augmentation (35 km³/yr central global output).

In 100,000 baseline runs, 18.460% (95% Wilson interval: 18.221-18.702%) reach sustained volumetric break-even by step 30 (2057) and 32.438% (32.149-32.729%) by step 50 (2077). Common-random-number isolation separates the two accounting ledgers: consumptive displacement alone (solar_food_only) reaches 16.638% by year 30 and 31.693% by year 50, whereas desalinated supply augmentation alone (desal_only) reaches 0.058% and 0.232%. Conditional on solar-food deployment (35.057% of baseline runs), 52.552% break even by year 30 and 92.101% by year 50, with deployment lag acting as the dominant rank correlate (0.8902). Under the isolated solar-food-only timing variants (p_desal = 0), conditional on solar-food deployment (n = 35,057), the central timing scenario (19 yr lag, 25 yr ramp) reaches sustained break-even in 50.575% of runs by year 30 and 93.046% by year 50. Deployment is forced only in the corresponding isolated 15×15 threshold-map experiments evaluated through year 50, which reach 87.52% across attribution and displacement (10 of 225 cells at least 50%) and 99.00% across timing (150 of 225 cells at least 50%).

2.Research question and scale check

The model addresses a conditional accounting question: under what combinations of deployment probability, commercialization timing, rebound, and causal attribution could water benefits credited to AI offset global operational AI compute water consumption over a 50-year projection?

At current baseline estimates, global AI operational water consumption sits three orders of magnitude below crop irrigation. Li et al. (2025) project 2027 global Scope 1 and Scope 2 AI water demand at 4.2-6.6 km³/yr of freshwater withdrawals and 0.38-0.60 km³/yr of water consumption. Broader industry assessments cover wider boundaries than AI operational compute alone: the International Energy Agency (IEA, 2025) estimates total global data-center water consumption (spanning primary energy supply and electricity generation, direct cooling, and chip manufacturing, rather than AI-only workloads) at roughly 560 billion L in 2023 rising to 1,200 billion L in 2030, while Xylem and Global Water Intelligence (2026) project a 129% increase across the broader AI-driven new economy (including data centers, semiconductor fabrication, and power generation) by 2050.

Against that compute baseline, Chukalla et al. (2025) report 1,080 km³/yr of blue-water consumption across 46 crops in 2020 (1,228 km³/yr including paddy flooding), consistent with Mekonnen and Hoekstra (2011), who placed blue water at roughly 12% of a 7,404 Gm³/yr total crop water footprint (about 888 km³/yr, or 0.89 trillion m³/yr) for 1996-2005. Dividing the 2020 crop central figure (1,080 km³/yr) by the 2027 AI consumption midpoint (0.49 km³/yr) yields a ratio near 2,200. That ratio functions strictly as an order-of-magnitude check across two distinct base years and boundaries, because local watershed scarcity prevents agricultural savings in one basin from replacing evaporative cooling losses in another.

3.Background and pathway framing

Assigning the entire water gain of a clean technology to AI would overstate AI's causal role, because materials research, capital cost declines, energy prices, and regulation drive adoption even without AI. The relevant quantity is the counterfactual fraction attributable to AI alongside the timeline over which a technology actually scales.

Two pathways represent distinct physical mechanisms:

  • Desalination (supply credit): Global operational desalination capacity totals 95.37 million m³/day (34.81 km³/yr across 15,906 plants; Jones et al., 2019). Reverse osmosis already operates close to thermodynamic energy limits (Elimelech and Phillip, 2011), constraining plausible cost-driven expansion. Because desalination produces new coastal freshwater rather than reducing evaporative consumption, it enters the volumetric ledger as an explicit supply credit.
  • Solar-driven microbial food ("solar food", consumptive displacement): Power-to-food microbial protein routes can produce protein using substantially less blue water per kilogram than conventional soybean crops (Sillman et al., 2020, 2022), and scenario modeling suggests microbial protein could reduce global cropland demand by 0-13% (central 6%) by 2050 when substituting for animal feed (Pikaar et al., 2018).

Because real-world deployment probabilities, 50-year rebound fractions, and counterfactual AI attribution shares cannot be measured empirically today, the model treats joint probabilities as exploratory priors and focuses on conditional outcomes, structural knockouts, sensitivity rankings, and break-even threshold maps.

5.Accounting scope, equations, and parameters

Basis separation, calendar mapping, and regional boundaries

Water withdrawals (total extracted volume) and water consumption (evaporated, transpired, or product-bound removal from the local basin) are never mixed within a scenario. Although configs/base.toml defaults to withdrawal, the global withdrawal-basis agricultural rows (A_solar_food and r_solar_food) carry MISSING sources in data/parameters.csv. All model runs here therefore use the sourced consumption basis. Five US compute rows informed by Shehabi et al. (2024; LBNL-2001637) reside in the catalog for reference, but region = "us" is blocked by validation because US-scale benefit baselines are absent.

By calendar convention, 2027 is the initial baseline reference year (t = 0), and simulation steps t = 1 through t = 50 represent calendar years 2028 through 2077 (50 discrete annual compounding increments mapped as calendar_year = 2027 + t). Because the global AI baseline of Li et al. (2025) already combines Scope 1 cooling and Scope 2 power-plant water into W_direct_global_consumption, the separate indirect row W_indirect_global_consumption is fixed at zero and indirect switch k has no effect on global runs. Scope 3 water embedded in chip fabrication and facility construction is excluded.

Supply credit versus consumptive offset: Desalination adds new coastal supply instead of preventing evaporative consumption. While the baseline ledger sums both pathways for completeness, the solar_food_only knockout and p_desal = 0 timing and threshold runs isolate the strict consumptive-displacement ledger, whereas desal_only isolates the supply-credit benchmark.

Governing equations

Across annual time steps t ∈ {1, ..., T}, compute water consumption C(t) compounds over two growth phases:

C(t) = (W_direct + k · W_indirect) · (1 + g1)min(t, G1) · (1 + g2)max(t − G1, 0)

Each benefit pathway i ∈ {desal, solar_food} draws a single Bernoulli deployment indicator si ~ Bernoulli(pi) per run, multiplied by constant counterfactual AI attribution share αi (modeled as a fixed scalar rather than an acceleration function), gross pathway potential, adoption ramp, and rebound offset:

Bi(t) = si · αi · Grossi · rampi(t) · (1 − ρi)
rampi(t) = 1[t > Li] if Ri = 0, else clip((t − Li) / Ri, 0, 1)
Gross_desal = D · θ  |  Gross_solar_food = φ · A · (1 − r)

Cumulative net balance through year t is N(t) = Στ=1..t (B_desal(τ) + B_solar_food(τ) − C(τ)). Sustained break-even year t* is the earliest step t ∈ [1, T] such that N(τ) ≥ 0 for every subsequent step τ ∈ [t, T]. Threshold analyses explicitly separate the reporting horizon H_rep (30 yr, by which cumulative balance must turn non-negative) from the evaluation horizon H_eval (50 yr in v0.3.0, with H_eval ≥ H_rep, through which the balance must remain non-negative). Temporary positive balances that revert below zero under later cost growth do not qualify. Runs without sustained break-even by step 50 are classified as never and coded as T + 1 = 51 in rank correlations. Reported 95% Wilson intervals describe finite-simulation uncertainty in the estimated proportions, conditional on the specified model and input distributions; they do not represent uncertainty in the underlying parameter assumptions, model structure, or real-world future outcomes.

Parameter catalog

Of the 29 rows in data/parameters.csv, 20 are active on the global consumption basis (Table 1; 2 global withdrawal rows, 2 MISSING withdrawal agricultural rows, and 5 reserved US rows are inactive). Compute growth assumptions (g_global, G_global, g2_global) are anchored to near-term IEA (2025) electricity and water figures (415 TWh in 2024 to 1,200 TWh in 2035; 560 billion L in 2023 to 1,200 billion L in 2030), Shehabi et al. (2024), and Xylem and Global Water Intelligence (2026), with no cited source covering 2035-2077. Desalination output D_desal annualizes the 95.37 million m³/day (~34.81 km³/yr) plant inventory in Jones et al. (2019) to a 35 km³/yr mode, while the 25-45 km³/yr spread is the author's judgment. Solar-food lag anchors to the April 2024 startup of Solar Foods' commercial Factory 01 (160 t/yr company-reported capacity; Solar Foods Oyj, 2025) shifted by historical energy-technology formative phases measured across 16 technologies (10 yr fastest, 22 yr mean; Bento and Wilson, 2016) alongside transition durations from Grubler et al. (2016) and Gross et al. (2018). Parameter notes for A_solar_food_consumption and r_solar_food_consumption record that source values came from secondary summaries and that the full corrected table in Sillman et al. (2022) was not directly checked.

6.Compute water trajectory and baseline balance

Under the two-phase cost function, median operational AI water consumption grows from 0.49 km³/yr in 2027 (t = 0) to 0.971 km³/yr by step 5 (2032, 1.99×), 1.081 km³/yr by step 10 (2037, 2.21×), 1.420 km³/yr by step 25 (2052, 2.92×), and 2.201 km³/yr by step 50 (2077, 4.53×; Table 2). At the 95th percentile, year-50 demand reaches 6.741 km³/yr (13.57×). At the 5th percentile, negative sampled g2_global values allow late-horizon demand to ease slightly from 0.695 km³/yr in 2052 to 0.621 km³/yr in 2077. Cumulative 50-year compute consumption spans 16.14-517.57 km³ across all runs (median 73.61 km³).

Because deployment delays accumulate deficits early while roughly two-thirds of runs fail to deploy solar food, median cumulative net balance N(t) stays negative across every benchmark step, shifting from −8.994 km³ at step 10 (2037) to −42.820 km³ at step 50 (2077; Table 2, fig3_net_balance_fan). Upper quantiles cross zero between steps 20 and 30, reaching +65.304 km³ at the 95th percentile by step 30 (2057) and +92.908 km³ at the 75th percentile (+407.162 km³ at the 95th) by step 50 (2077).

Overall, 2 runs (0.002%, 95% Wilson CI: 0.001-0.007%) achieve sustained break-even by step 10 (too sparse for stable inference), 3,782 (3.782%, 3.666-3.902%) by step 20, 18,460 (18.460%, 18.221-18.702%) by step 30, and 32,438 (32.438%, 32.149-32.729%) by step 50, while 67,562 (67.562%) never reach sustained break-even within 50 steps (fig1_breakeven_dist, fig2_p_breakeven_horizon, fig5_scope_comparison). Successful runs break even at a median of step 29 (calendar year 2056; 5th-95th percentile: 18-44 yr). Five baseline runs cross N(t) ≥ 0 temporarily and revert below zero before step 50, including three runs that remain non-negative at step 30 before late Phase 2 compute growth drives N(50) < 0.

7.Consumption offsets versus supply credits

The two pathways differ in physical scale by more than an order of magnitude. Assuming each pathway deploys (si = 1) and reaches full ramp-up (ramp = 1) at catalog parameter modes gives mature annual benefits of 0.236 km³/yr for desalination (35 × 0.05 × 0.15 × 0.90) versus 14.434 km³/yr for solar food (1,080 × 0.06 × 0.99 × 0.25 × 0.90), roughly a 61-to-1 ratio; these are conditional point illustrations rather than expected annual benefits across all simulations. Across Monte Carlo runs where each pathway deploys, year-50 realized medians are 0.314 km³/yr for desalination (0.107-0.738 km³/yr) and 13.38 km³/yr for solar food (3.77-29.97 km³/yr). Aggregated over all runs and years, solar food generates 91.032% of total credited volume and desalination generates 8.968%.

Isolating each pathway via CRN knockouts separates the consumptive ledger from the supply credit (Table 3, fig6_knockouts). Restricting benefits solely to consumptive crop displacement (solar_food_only) yields sustained break-even in 16.638% (16.408-16.870%) of runs by year 30 and 31.693% (31.405-31.982%) by year 50. Isolating desalination alone (desal_only) succeeds in 0.058% (0.045-0.075%) of runs by year 30 and 0.232% (0.204-0.264%) by year 50 (median N(50) = −64.299 km³).

Conditional subsets do not isolate pathways: In conditional.csv, runs where s_desal = 1 break even in 19.051% of cases by year 30 and 32.590% by year 50 only because solar food deploys independently in 34.97% (25,326 / 72,425) of those runs. Likewise, desalination can co-deploy independently in the s_solar_food = 1 subset. Use desal_only and solar_food_only for isolated pathway attribution.

Among the 35,057 baseline runs where solar food deploys (35.057%, matching its 0.35 uniform mean), 52.552% (52.029-53.074%) reach sustained break-even by year 30 and 92.101% (91.814-92.379%) by year 50 (fig8_breakeven_given_solar_food). Comparing CRN structural overrides measures how individual assumptions alter outcomes: forcing full AI attribution (alpha_1) lifts 30-year break-even by 35.993 percentage points to 54.453%, with a 50-year break-even proportion of 59.080% and 40.920% of runs failing to achieve sustained break-even (deployment remains stochastic in this scenario). Forcing full deployment (success_1) matches that 30-year mark (54.402%) while lifting 50-year break-even to 92.779% (median N(50) = +181.219 km³). Removing lags and ramps (no_delay) triggers immediate step benefits from t = 1 (median t* = 1 yr, 35.210% by year 30). Freezing compute growth (flat_cost) reaches 31.308% by year 30 and 40.027% by year 50, removing rebound (no_rebound) adds 2.472 percentage points at year 30 (20.932%), and the combined ceiling case reaches 97.666% by year 30 and 99.934% by year 50.

8.Solar-food timing scenarios

9.Two-parameter threshold surfaces

Four 15 × 15 threshold grids (5,000 shared base draws per cell; 1,125,000 cell evaluations per map) evaluate sustained break-even by reporting horizon H_rep = 30 years while requiring balances to stay non-negative through evaluation horizon H_eval = 50 years (Table 5). Panels 7a and 7c run the joint model with random deployment probabilities left intact; panels 7b and 7d isolate solar food (p_desal = 0) and force deployment (--conditional solar_food).

On the joint attribution-displacement surface (Figure 7a), even the upper grid corner (α_solar_food = 0.50, φ_solar_food = 0.13) reaches only 31.10% (29.83-32.40%), with 19 of 225 cells reaching 10% and zero reaching 50%, because solar food fails to deploy in roughly two-thirds of runs. Under the isolated conditional grid (p_desal = 0, forced deployment; Figure 7b), the same upper corner reaches 87.52% (86.58-88.41%), with 10 cells exceeding 50% and 3 exceeding 75% (the lower corner at 0.001, 0.001 falls to 0% without desalination). Note that α_solar_food < 0.10 probes below the baseline triangular lower bound as a stress test.

Timing grids reveal the same contrast (Figures 7c and 7d). At immediate adoption (L = 0, R = 0), joint unconditional break-even tops out at 34.34% (33.04-35.67%; 0 cells reaching 50%), whereas isolated conditional break-even reaches 99.00% (98.68-99.24%; 150 of 225 cells at least 50%, 100 cells at least 90%). When lag reaches L = 30 yr, solar-food benefits remain zero at step 30 under t > L, dropping isolated break-even to 0% (versus 0.08% in the joint grid via desalination).

10.Sensitivity and outcome conditions

Comparing pooled and conditional rank correlations separates binary deployment gating from timing and scale drivers (Table 6). Across all 100,000 runs, the binary deployment draw s_solar_food dominates abs_corr_breakeven (0.9266, tripping breakeven_dominance_warning above 0.85). Because s_solar_food mediates p_solar_food in that pooled regression, p_solar_food drops to 0.0012 in abs_corr_breakeven despite ranking second in terminal balance PRCC (+0.2748).

Evaluating partial rank correlations strictly inside the deployed subset (s_solar_food = 1, n = 35,057, noting that desalination still deploys independently in that subset) removes that mediation artifact and exposes the ordering of parameters governing when break-even occurs: lag L_solar_food leads at 0.8902, followed by displacement share φ_solar_food (0.7543), attribution α_solar_food (0.5638), ramp R_solar_food (0.5176), Phase 1 duration G_global (0.5064), and Phase 2 growth g2_global (0.4458). For terminal net balance N(50), no continuous parameter exceeds 0.85 (g2_global is largest at −0.3816). In deterministic tornado swings around the expected-value midpoint baseline (+34.441 km³; fig4_tornado), φ_solar_food (207.973 km³), α_solar_food (153.580 km³), p_solar_food (137.125 km³), g2_global (132.922 km³), and L_solar_food (114.175 km³) generate the widest swings (with W_indirect = 0 by construction).

11.What the code, figure, and audit checks resolved

Engineering and provenance passes across revisions 7c5b862, 0.2.0, and v0.3.0 resolved analytical, CLI, citation, and figure-interpretation gaps while leaving the core physical equations unchanged:

  • plot_threshold_map was updated to apply log axes when --log is set and filter contour levels against the data range so surfaces peaking below 0.5 emit no warnings.
  • compute_threshold_map and cmd_threshold gained --conditional, --eval-horizon (defaulting to model horizon T = 50 in v0.3.0), explicit horizon validation (H_eval ≥ H_rep > 0 and bounded by T), and strict rejection of --samples so cell counts are controlled unambiguously via --n-per-cell.
  • compute_sourcing_priority added abs_corr_breakeven_given_solar_food, breakeven_dominance_warning, and an explicit disclosure in rank_notice that desalination deploys independently inside the solar-food-conditioned subset.
  • compute_tornado was updated to use arithmetic midpoints on uniform distributions and geometric medians on lognormal distributions rather than arbitrary catalog mode entries.
  • cmd_knockouts was converted to streaming evaluation (~8/9 peak-memory reduction), cmd_figures gained --horizon support and skipped redundant global k = 1 runs, cmd_reproduce exposed all --threshold-* flags, and all simulation subcommands (run, figures, knockouts, threshold, and reproduce) now write run_metadata.json with software_version, baseline_year, and cli_arguments.
  • simulate.py added explicit calendar_year columns (2027 + t) to cost_trajectory.csv, net_balance_by_horizon.csv, and conditions.csv, matching the documented 2027 baseline and 2028-2077 step convention.
  • Inspection of SVG text labels in results_final_v0.3.0.zip clarified two figure points: fig5_scope_comparison displays only the combined Scope 1+2 curve in global runs (rather than comparing Scope 1 against Scope 2), and the red PLACEHOLDER INPUTS - NOT FOR PUBLICATION footer stamp overlaps x-axis labels and threshold map interiors in PNG plots without affecting CSV values.

12.Limitations

The thirteen model boundaries documented in docs/model.md constrain interpretation across every simulated table (Table 8):

Table 8. Methodological boundaries documented in docs/model.md.

# Limitation Implication for interpreting model outputs
1 Spatial aggregation Global volumetric balances cannot match agricultural savings in one watershed to data-center cooling consumption in a different stressed basin
2 No scarcity weighting Cubic meters are summed equally without weighting by baseline water stress, aquifer depletion, or seasonal flows
3 Excluded Scope 3 water Upstream water embedded in semiconductor fabrication, server supply chains, and facility construction is omitted from C(t)
4 Post-2035 growth gap No empirical projection supports g2_global between 2035 and 2077, which spans most of the 50-year horizon
5 Static baselines Crop blue-water use A (2020) and desalinated output D (2019) remain constant without climate, population, or dietary feedbacks
6 Exploratory boundary Outputs represent conditional distributions under exploratory priors rather than forecasts
7 No discounting Future cubic meters and early deficits carry equal volumetric weight
8 Independent sampling Latin hypercube sampling omits cross-parameter correlations (such as faster growth driving higher rebound or R&D spending)
9 Zero-benefit notice A basis_notice flags any pathway whose gross benefit is identically zero; none triggers under active consumption inputs
10 Unmodeled AI-system footprint Water embedded in sensors, inference, or desalination power beyond r_solar_food_consumption is excluded
11 Binary deployment ceiling One Bernoulli draw per pathway bounds unconditional break-even by the joint deployment probability, omitting partial regional adoption
12 Desalination supply credit Desalination adds coastal supply rather than cutting consumption; use solar_food_only to isolate consumptive savings
13 Energy-diffusion analogies L_solar_food and R_solar_food transfer energy-sector diffusion timescales to food systems, while φ maps cropland share onto blue water

Two empirical caveats in data/parameters.csv also remain open for future primary verification: cropland area displacement from Pikaar et al. (2018) overstates blue-water savings wherever displaced feed crops rely on green rainwater (~88% of total crop water footprint in Mekonnen and Hoekstra, 2011), and the corrected reference table in Sillman et al. (2022) was not directly inspected beyond the corrected soybean comparator.

13.Scientific status and interpretive bounds

Because deployment probabilities, counterfactual AI attribution shares, rebound fractions, and desalination cost-driven expansion rates remain unverified placeholders, this study does not support any claim about:

  • The real-world probability that AI will repay or offset its operational water consumption
  • Whether AI causally accelerates desalination or solar-food commercialization
  • Basin-level hydrological adequacy or local cooling water neutrality
  • Realized water intensity of commercial solar-food plants beyond the single 160 t/yr initial factory benchmark

What the frozen model establishes structurally is narrower: given the physical scale of global desalination (35 km³/yr) versus crop blue water (1,080 km³/yr), under the selected placeholder distributions and parameter ranges the desalination-only scenario produces very little sustained break-even, reaching 0.232% by year 50. By contrast, agricultural displacement depends on arriving early (reaching 50.575% at 19 yr lag and 98.172% at 7 yr lag under isolated p_desal = 0 conditional runs) and achieving large attributed crop-water displacement (10 of 225 cells at least 50% across the isolated attribution-displacement grid).
