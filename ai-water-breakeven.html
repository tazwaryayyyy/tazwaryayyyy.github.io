1.Abstract

Evaluating whether water savings or supply gains linked to artificial intelligence (AI) can offset the operational water footprint of AI compute requires separating physical scale limits from unmeasured counterfactual assumptions. This study implements a 50-year global consumptive water ledger evaluated over 100,000 Latin hypercube samples (seed = 42). Operational Scope 1 and Scope 2 compute consumption starts at 0.38-0.60 km³/yr in 2027 and compounds under a two-phase trajectory. Two benefit pathways are tested against that demand: crop blue-water displacement via solar-driven microbial food production (1,080 km³/yr central agricultural baseline) and coastal desalinated supply augmentation (35 km³/yr central global output).

In 100,000 baseline runs, 18.46% (95% Wilson interval: 18.22-18.70%) reach sustained volumetric break-even by year 30 and 32.44% (32.15-32.73%) by year 50. Common-random-number isolation separates the two accounting ledgers: consumptive displacement alone (solar_food_only) reaches 16.64% by year 30 and 31.69% by year 50, whereas desalinated supply augmentation alone (desal_only) reaches 0.058% and 0.232%. Conditional on solar-food deployment (35.06% of baseline runs), 52.55% break even by year 30 and 92.10% by year 50, with deployment lag acting as the dominant rank correlate (0.890). Conditional two-parameter threshold maps evaluated through year 50 indicate that a 50% break-even share by year 30 requires an AI-attributed crop-water displacement product α · φ near 0.014 under baseline timing, and that break-even falls sharply once deployment lags exceed 20-24 yr.

2.Research question and scale check

The model addresses a conditional accounting question: under what combinations of deployment probability, commercialization timing, rebound, and causal attribution could water benefits credited to AI offset global operational AI compute water consumption over a 50-year horizon?

At current baseline estimates, global AI operational water consumption sits three orders of magnitude below crop irrigation. Li et al. (2025) project 2027 global Scope 1 and Scope 2 AI water demand at 4.2-6.6 km³/yr of freshwater withdrawals and 0.38-0.60 km³/yr of water consumption. Broader industry assessments that include electricity supply chains and chip fabrication project continued growth: the International Energy Agency (IEA, 2025) reports global data-center water consumption at roughly 560 billion L in 2023 rising to 1,200 billion L in 2030, while Xylem and Global Water Intelligence (2026) project a 129% increase across the AI value chain by 2050.

Against that compute baseline, Chukalla et al. (2025) report 1,080 km³/yr of blue-water consumption across 46 crops in 2020 (1,228 km³/yr including paddy flooding), consistent with Mekonnen and Hoekstra (2011), who placed blue water at roughly 12% of a 7,404 Gm³/yr total crop water footprint (0.89 trillion m³/yr) for 1996-2005. Dividing the 2020 crop central figure (1,080 km³/yr) by the 2027 AI consumption midpoint (0.49 km³/yr) yields a ratio near 2,200. That ratio functions strictly as an order-of-magnitude check across two distinct base years and boundaries, because local watershed scarcity prevents agricultural savings in one basin from replacing evaporative cooling losses in another.

3.Background and pathway framing

Assigning the entire water gain of a clean technology to AI would overstate AI's causal role, because materials research, capital cost declines, energy prices, and regulation drive adoption even without AI. The relevant quantity is the counterfactual fraction attributable to AI alongside the timeline over which a technology actually scales.

Two pathways represent distinct physical mechanisms:

  • Desalination (supply credit): Global operational desalination capacity totals 95.37 million m³/day (34.81 km³/yr across 15,906 plants; Jones et al., 2019). Reverse osmosis already operates close to thermodynamic energy limits (Elimelech and Phillip, 2011), constraining plausible cost-driven expansion. Because desalination produces new coastal freshwater rather than reducing evaporative consumption, it enters the volumetric ledger as an explicit supply credit.
  • Solar-driven microbial food ("solar food", consumptive displacement): Power-to-food microbial protein routes can produce protein using substantially less blue water per kilogram than conventional soybean crops (Sillman et al., 2020, 2022), and scenario modeling suggests microbial protein could reduce global cropland demand by 0-13% (central 6%) by 2050 when substituting for animal feed (Pikaar et al., 2018).

Because real-world deployment probabilities, 50-year rebound fractions, and counterfactual AI attribution shares cannot be measured empirically today, the model treats joint probabilities as exploratory priors and focuses on conditional outcomes, structural knockouts, sensitivity rankings, and break-even threshold maps.

5.Accounting scope, equations, and parameters

Basis separation and regional boundaries

Water withdrawals (total extracted volume) and water consumption (evaporated, transpired, or product-bound removal from the local basin) are never mixed within a scenario. Although configs/base.toml defaults to withdrawal, the global withdrawal-basis agricultural rows (A_solar_food and r_solar_food) carry MISSING sources in data/parameters.csv. All model runs here therefore use the sourced consumption basis. Five US compute rows informed by Shehabi et al. (2024; LBNL-2001637) reside in the catalog for reference, but region = "us" is blocked by validation because US-scale benefit baselines are absent.

Because the global AI baseline of Li et al. (2025) already combines Scope 1 cooling and Scope 2 power-plant water into W_direct_global_consumption, the separate indirect row W_indirect_global_consumption is fixed at zero and indirect switch k has no effect on global runs. Scope 3 water embedded in chip fabrication and facility construction is excluded.

Supply credit versus consumptive offset: Desalination adds new coastal supply instead of preventing evaporative consumption. While the baseline ledger sums both pathways for completeness, the solar_food_only knockout provides the clean consumptive-displacement ledger and desal_only provides the isolated supply-credit benchmark.

Governing equations

Across annual time steps t ∈ {1, ..., T} (where t = 0 is 2027 and t = 50 is 2077), compute water consumption C(t) compounds over two growth phases:

C(t) = (W_direct + k · W_indirect) · (1 + g1)min(t, G1) · (1 + g2)max(t − G1, 0)

Each benefit pathway i ∈ {desal, solar_food} draws a single Bernoulli deployment indicator si ~ Bernoulli(pi) per run, multiplied by AI attribution share αi, gross pathway potential, adoption ramp, and rebound offset:

Bi(t) = si · αi · Grossi · rampi(t) · (1 − ρi)
rampi(t) = 1[t > Li] if Ri = 0, else clip((t − Li) / Ri, 0, 1)
Gross_desal = D · θ  |  Gross_solar_food = φ · A · (1 − r)

Cumulative net balance through year t is N(t) = Στ=1..t (B_desal(τ) + B_solar_food(τ) − C(τ)). Sustained break-even year t* is the earliest step t ∈ [1, T] such that N(τ) ≥ 0 for every subsequent step τ ∈ [t, T]. Temporary positive balances that revert below zero under later cost growth do not qualify. Runs without sustained break-even by year 50 are classified as never and coded as T + 1 = 51 in rank correlations.

6.Compute water trajectory and baseline balance

Under the two-phase cost function, median operational AI water consumption grows from 0.49 km³/yr in 2027 to 0.97 km³/yr by year 5 (1.99×), 1.08 km³/yr by year 10 (2.21×), 1.42 km³/yr by year 25 (2.92×), and 2.20 km³/yr by year 50 (4.53×; Table 2). At the 95th percentile, year-50 demand reaches 6.74 km³/yr (13.57×). Cumulative 50-year compute consumption spans 16.14-517.57 km³ across all runs (median 73.61 km³).

Because deployment delays accumulate deficits early while roughly two-thirds of runs fail to deploy solar food, median cumulative net balance N(t) stays negative across every benchmark year, shifting from −8.99 km³ at year 10 to −42.82 km³ at year 50 (Table 2; fig3_net_balance_fan). Upper quantiles cross zero between years 20 and 30, reaching +65.30 km³ at the 95th percentile by year 30 and +92.91 km³ at the 75th percentile (+407.16 km³ at the 95th) by year 50.

Overall, 2 runs (0.002%, 95% Wilson CI: 0.0005-0.0073%) achieve sustained break-even by year 10, 3,782 (3.78%, 3.67-3.90%) by year 20, 18,460 (18.46%, 18.22-18.70%) by year 30, and 32,438 (32.44%, 32.15-32.73%) by year 50, while 67,562 (67.56%) never reach sustained break-even within 50 years (fig1_breakeven_dist, fig2_p_breakeven_horizon, fig5_scope_comparison). Successful runs break even at a median of year 29 (5th-95th percentile: 18-44 yr). Five baseline runs cross N(t) ≥ 0 temporarily and revert below zero before year 50, including three runs that remain non-negative at year 30 before late Phase 2 compute growth drives N(50) < 0.

7.Consumption offsets versus supply credits

The two pathways differ in physical scale by more than an order of magnitude. When deployed, desalination reaches a year-50 plateau credit of 0.314 km³/yr at the median (5th-95th: 0.107-0.738 km³/yr), below 2027 AI water demand (0.49 km³/yr) and roughly one-seventh of median year-50 demand (2.20 km³/yr). When deployed, solar food reaches a year-50 displacement of 13.38 km³/yr at the median (3.77-29.97 km³/yr), more than 42 times larger. Across all runs and years, solar food generates 91.03% of total credited volume and desalination generates 8.97%.

Isolating each pathway via CRN knockouts separates the consumptive ledger from the supply credit (Table 3, fig6_knockouts). Restricting benefits solely to consumptive crop displacement (solar_food_only) yields sustained break-even in 16.64% (16.41-16.87%) of runs by year 30 and 31.69% (31.41-31.98%) by year 50. Isolating desalination alone (desal_only) succeeds in 0.058% (0.045-0.075%) of runs by year 30 and 0.232% (0.204-0.264%) by year 50 (median N(50) = −64.30 km³).

Conditional subsets do not isolate pathways: In conditional.csv, runs where s_desal = 1 break even in 19.05% of cases by year 30 and 32.59% by year 50 only because solar food deploys independently in 34.97% (25,326 / 72,425) of those runs. Use desal_only and solar_food_only for isolated pathway attribution.

Among the 35,057 baseline runs where solar food deploys (35.06%, matching its 0.35 uniform mean), 52.55% (52.03-53.07%) reach sustained break-even by year 30 and 92.10% (91.81-92.38%) by year 50 (fig8_breakeven_given_solar_food). Comparing CRN structural overrides measures how individual assumptions alter outcomes: forcing full AI attribution (alpha_1) lifts 30-year break-even by 35.99 percentage points to 54.45%, though 50-year success stops at 59.08% because 40.92% of runs never deploy solar food. Forcing full deployment (success_1) matches that 30-year mark (54.40%) while lifting 50-year break-even to 92.78% (median N(50) = +181.22 km³). Removing lags and ramps (no_delay) triggers immediate step benefits from t = 1 (median t* = 1 yr, 35.21% by year 30). Freezing compute growth (flat_cost) reaches 31.31% by year 30 and 40.03% by year 50, removing rebound (no_rebound) adds 2.47 percentage points at year 30 (20.93%), and the combined ceiling case reaches 97.67% by year 30 and 99.93% by year 50.

8.Solar-food timing scenarios

Fixing solar-food lag L_solar_food and ramp R_solar_food to fast (7 and 10 yr), central (19 and 25 yr), and slow (30 and 45 yr) schedules separates near-term payback from late-century payback (Table 4). 9.Two-parameter threshold surfaces

Two 15 × 15 threshold grids (5,000 shared base draws per cell) evaluate sustained break-even by year 30 while tracking trajectories through eval_horizon = 50 years to catch late reversals (fig7_threshold_map, Table 5). The conditional surfaces (--conditional solar_food) isolate the physical parameter thresholds from the random 34.62% deployment draw (1,731 / 5,000) present in the shared base matrix.

On the conditional α_solar_food × φ_solar_food log-spaced grid, break-even tracks the attributed displacement product α · φ. The 10% probability contour runs near α · φ ≈ 0.0043-0.0045 (9.42% at 0.132 × 0.0324 = 0.00427; 10.42% at 0.0349 × 0.130 = 0.00453). The 50% contour sits near α · φ ≈ 0.014 (48.04% at 0.2058 × 0.0649 = 0.01335; 51.78% at 0.3208 × 0.0458 = 0.01469), corresponding to roughly 13.5 km³/yr of attributed annual plateau displacement at central catalog values of A, r, and ρ. At the upper grid corner (0.50 × 0.13 = 0.065), conditional break-even reaches 89.40% (88.52-90.22%).

On the conditional L_solar_food × R_solar_food grid, zero ramp (R = 0) keeps 30-year break-even above 90% up to L = 23.57 yr (91.54%) and at 64.56% at L = 27.86 yr before dropping to 0.10% at L = 30 yr. With a 22.5 yr ramp, break-even crosses 50% between L = 19.29 yr (61.52%) and L = 21.43 yr (38.96%), dropping to 2.16% at L = 25.71 yr. With a 45 yr ramp, break-even drops below 50% at L = 17.14 yr (43.42%).

Why unconditional surfaces cap below 35%: Leaving p_solar_food active in unconditional threshold grids restricts solar-food deployment to 34.62% of the 5,000 draws per cell, capping maximum probability at 31.10% on α × φ and 34.34% on L × R. Verifying stability through eval_horizon = 50 also catches slight late-horizon reversals compared with a 30-year-only evaluation (34.34% versus 34.52% at L = 0, R = 0).

10.Sensitivity and dominance diagnostics

Comparing pooled and conditional rank correlations separates binary deployment gating from timing and scale drivers (Table 6). Across all 100,000 runs, the binary deployment draw s_solar_food dominates abs_corr_breakeven (0.927, tripping breakeven_dominance_warning above 0.85). Because s_solar_food mediates p_solar_food in that pooled regression, p_solar_food drops to 0.0012 in abs_corr_breakeven despite ranking second in terminal balance PRCC (+0.275).

Evaluating partial rank correlations strictly inside the deployed subset (s_solar_food = 1, n = 35,057) removes that mediation artifact and exposes the ordering of parameters governing when break-even occurs: lag L_solar_food leads at 0.890, followed by displacement share φ_solar_food (0.754), attribution α_solar_food (0.564), ramp R_solar_food (0.518), Phase 1 duration G_global (0.506), and Phase 2 growth g2_global (0.446). For terminal net balance N(50), no continuous parameter exceeds 0.85 (g2_global is largest at −0.382). In deterministic tornado swings around the expected-value midpoint baseline (+34.44 km³; fig4_tornado), φ_solar_food (207.97 km³), α_solar_food (153.58 km³), p_solar_food (137.12 km³), g2_global (132.92 km³), and L_solar_food (114.17 km³) generate the widest swings.

11.What the code and citation audits resolved

Pre-freeze source audits between baseline revision 7c5b862 and frozen tag 0.2.0 (bfcfffc) repaired several silent analytical and provenance defects without changing underlying Monte Carlo distributions:

  • plot_threshold_map previously ignored --log and crashed or warned when probability ceilings fell below hardcoded 0.5 and 0.9 contour levels; log axes and data-range contour filtering were added.
  • compute_threshold_map gained --conditional and --eval-horizon so physical thresholds can be evaluated free of random deployment caps and checked for post-30-year reversals.
  • compute_sourcing_priority gained abs_corr_breakeven_given_solar_food and breakeven_dominance_warning to surface conditional timing rank correlations and binary mediation.
  • compute_tornado previously read arbitrary catalog mode entries on uniform distributions (0.80 instead of 0.725 for p_desal; 0.30 instead of 0.35 for p_solar_food) and now uses distribution midpoints.
  • cmd_knockouts was converted to streaming evaluation (~8/9 peak-memory reduction), cmd_figures gained --horizon support and skipped redundant global k = 1 runs, cmd_reproduce capped threshold horizon leakage, and file descriptors were closed via context managers.
  • data/parameters.csv and docs/model.md were updated to cite Bento and Wilson (2016) for the 22-year formative-phase mean, Jones et al. (2019) for global desalinated output D_desal, Shehabi et al. (2024) and IEA (2025) water figures on growth rows, and GitHub-compatible MathJax blocks.
  • Visual inspection of generated PNG figures confirmed readable axes, legends, and log ticks, while flagging a cosmetic layout overlap where the red PLACEHOLDER INPUTS - NOT FOR PUBLICATION footer stamp intersects x-axis labels and threshold grid interiors.

12.Limitations

The thirteen model boundaries documented in docs/model.md constrain interpretation across every simulated table:

# Limitation Implication for interpreting model outputs
1 Spatial aggregation Global volumetric balances cannot match agricultural savings in one watershed to data-center cooling consumption in a different stressed basin
2 No scarcity weighting Cubic meters are summed equally without weighting by baseline water stress, aquifer depletion, or seasonal flows
3 Excluded Scope 3 water Upstream water embedded in semiconductor fabrication, server supply chains, and facility construction is omitted from C(t)
4 Post-2035 growth gap No empirical projection supports g2_global between 2035 and 2077, which spans most of the 50-year horizon
5 Static baselines Crop blue-water use A (2020) and desalinated output D (2019) remain constant without climate, population, or dietary feedbacks
6 Exploratory boundary Outputs represent conditional distributions under exploratory priors rather than forecasts
7 No discounting Future cubic meters and early deficits carry equal volumetric weight
8 Independent sampling Latin hypercube sampling omits cross-parameter correlations (such as faster growth driving higher rebound or R&D spending)
9 Zero-benefit notice A basis_notice flags any pathway whose gross benefit is identically zero; none triggers under active consumption inputs
10 Unmodeled AI-system footprint Water embedded in sensors, inference, or desalination power beyond r_solar_food_consumption is excluded
11 Binary deployment ceiling One Bernoulli draw per pathway bounds unconditional break-even by the joint deployment probability, omitting partial regional adoption
12 Desalination supply credit Desalination adds coastal supply rather than cutting consumption; use solar_food_only to isolate consumptive savings
13 Energy-diffusion analogies L_solar_food and R_solar_food transfer energy-sector diffusion timescales to food systems, while φ maps cropland share onto blue water

Two additional empirical caveats apply to the underlying catalog: cropland area displacement from Pikaar et al. (2018) overstates blue-water savings wherever displaced feed crops rely on green rainwater (~88% of total crop water footprint in Mekonnen and Hoekstra, 2011), and the full corrected reference table in Sillman et al. (2022) was not directly inspected beyond the corrected soybean comparator.

13.Scientific status and interpretive bounds

Because deployment probabilities, counterfactual AI attribution shares, rebound fractions, and desalination cost-driven expansion rates remain unverified placeholders, this study does not support any claim about:

  • The real-world probability that AI will repay or offset its operational water consumption
  • Whether AI causally accelerates desalination or solar-food commercialization
  • Basin-level hydrological adequacy or local cooling water neutrality
  • Realized water intensity of commercial solar-food plants beyond the single 160 t/yr initial factory benchmark

What the frozen model establishes structurally is narrower: given the physical scale of global desalination (35 km³/yr) versus crop blue water (1,080 km³/yr), desalinated efficiency gains alone cannot offset multi-cubic-kilometer compute demand under plausible attribution shares, whereas agricultural displacement depends critically on arriving within roughly 20-24 yr and achieving an attributed displacement product α · φ near 0.014.
